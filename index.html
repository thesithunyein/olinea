Post-quantum authorization · Arc mainnet

USDC that answers to a hash-based key, and nothing else

Olinea holds USDC on Arc and releases it only for an SLH-DSA signature that Arc's own precompile verifies on-chain. No owner, no admin, no pause, no upgrade.

Hash-based, not elliptic-curve

32 bytes of public key. Breaking ECDSA does not forge a release.

Checked on-chain

Arc's precompile verifies inside the transaction. No oracle, no signer to trust.

No owner, no admin

Nothing can pause, upgrade or redirect it. Depositing grants no rights.

How it works

  1. Deposit. A vault is deployed with a 32-byte hash-based key and holds USDC. Anyone can deposit; nobody gains control.
  2. Sign. Your key signs one 32-byte digest binding chain, vault, recipient, amount and nonce. No Arc account needed.
  3. Relay. Anyone pays the gas. The vault decodes the boolean the precompile returns, and only then moves the USDC.

Verified against Arc mainnet

Real calls to the precompile at 0x1800…0004 with a real keypair. A forged signature is a value, not an exception.

Limitations

  • Unaudited. New code. A test suite is evidence, not a review. Don't deposit what you can't lose.
  • No recovery. Lose the key and the funds are gone by design — there is no escape hatch.
  • Circle's denylist still applies. USDC can block transfers. This is not denylist resistance.
  • Verification costs 382,879 gas — about 1,300× a plain transfer, and still $0.008.
  • Signatures are 7,856 bytes. Signing takes seconds, not milliseconds.

FAQ

Is Olinea audited?

No. A Foundry suite and a conformance run against the live precompile exist instead of a review — treat the vault accordingly.

What happens if I lose the signing key?

The funds are unrecoverable. No owner, no recovery address, no pause, no upgrade path — the key fixed at deployment is the only way out.

Does this protect me from quantum computers today?

Not today: nothing yet breaks ECDSA. The problem is that long-lived keys and recorded transactions are already being collected to be broken later.

What does a release cost?

382,879 gas. At Arc's pinned 20 Gwei and gas paid in USDC, about $0.0077 — roughly 1,300× a plain transfer, and still fractions of a cent.

Can I use this in my own contract?

Yes. MIT-licensed, and the pieces separate cleanly. The one rule to carry across: decode the boolean the precompile returns. The docs cover the digest, the ABI and the failure modes.
