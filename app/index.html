A vault only you can open.

Back up 24 words. They derive an SLH-DSA-SHA2-128s key that never leaves this tab and never crosses a network. The vault holds USDC on Arc, has no owner, no admin and no upgrade path, and will move money for exactly one thing: a signature from that key. No gas is required to try any of it — the proof step below runs against Arc mainnet for free.

1

Back up the key

These words are the vault. There is no recovery, no reset and no support line — if they are lost, the USDC is unspendable by anyone, including us. Write them on paper.

Words are checked locally. Nothing is sent anywhere.

One backup opens as many vaults as you like — the index is mixed into the derivation, so vault 0 and vault 1 are unrelated keys. Leave it at 0 unless you know why you are changing it.

2

Prove the key on Arc mainnet

Arc ships a post-quantum precompile. Hand it your verifying key, a message and a signature, and it answers with a boolean on real mainnet state. This is the exact call the vault makes before it moves a cent, so proving it here costs nothing and settles the question.

Two transactions: an approval, then the deposit. Once it is in, the only thing that can move it is a signed authorization — including for you.

4

Authorize a payment

The vault signs over one 32-byte digest that binds the chain id, the vault, the recipient, the amount and a nonce. Replay it anywhere else and it is worthless. Sign it here, check it against Arc's precompile before spending gas, and the release cannot fail on the signature.

To take your own money back out, name yourself as the recipient. There is no admin shortcut around this step, which is the entire point.

What is not true yet. The vault and the factory are written, tested and public, but neither is deployed on Arc mainnet — deploying costs about two cents of gas that this project does not have. Everything above that touches mainnet (the precompile proof, the balances, the digest cross-check) is real. Everything that needs a vault is real code waiting on that address, and the pre-flight check is why you will not waste gas when it is there.

  • Unaudited. Written to be small enough to read in one sitting, which is not the same thing.
  • Arc's PQ precompile answers false for a bad signature instead of reverting. The vault reads that boolean; a version that only checked that the call succeeded would be open.
  • Averaging 382,879 gas per verification — about 1,300× a plain USDC transfer — and a 7,856-byte signature. Post-quantum is not free.
  • USDC on Arc still honours Circle's denylist. A key that survives quantum computers does not protect you from a freeze.
  • The throwaway account is a private key in this browser's localStorage. Fund it with pocket change and nothing else.
